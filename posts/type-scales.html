Type scales that stay out of the way

May 30, 2026 · 4 min read · Kairo

A type scale is a set of decisions you make once so you do not make them on every page. The trick is to keep it small enough that you can remember it without looking it up.

Three sizes, plus a hero

Body text, a subheading and a heading cover almost everything. A single oversized hero is allowed because it appears once. Beyond that, more sizes create more questions.

body    16px   line-height 1.7
subhead 22px   line-height 1.3
heading 34px   line-height 1.2
hero    clamp(2.4rem, 6vw, 4.2rem)

Rhythm comes from spacing

Readable pages are mostly consistent gaps. Set a few spacing values and reuse them; the eye notices a broken rhythm faster than it notices a slightly different font size.

More white space is usually a design decision, not a missing element.

Small labels need care

Kickers and metadata can be small, but they still need contrast and a little letter-spacing. On this site they are uppercase, muted and short — just enough to name a section before the heading arrives.

Wrap up

Three sizes, a few spacing values and readable labels. The rest is restraint.
